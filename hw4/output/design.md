# Campus Customs — Design (Problem 10)

**Concept: a varsity-letterman storefront.** Black-and-pink stays the brand. On top of it I layered the things you'd see in a real campus shop: chenille patches, pennants, swing tags, a polaroid on the corkboard, and a store mascot who texts you back. Code lives in `frontend/src/design.css`, plus small components (`Ticker`, `Sticker`, `Reveal`, `flyToBag`).

| Area | What changed | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts** | **Graduate** (varsity block letters) for the brand, labels and prices. **Fraunces** (editorial serif, italic) for headlines. **DM Sans** for body and UI. | Instantly reads as "college athletics" rather than a generic template. Three clear roles make the hierarchy obvious: what it is, what it costs, the details. |
| **Colours and texture** | Same black and hot pink, plus a **cream** "paper" colour behind product photos and the polaroid, a soft pink radial glow in the top corner, and a faint **film-grain** overlay. | Cream makes the garment photos look like printed swing tags, not white boxes on black. Grain makes the black feel like fabric. Pink is kept for actions and prices, so the eye goes to what to click. |
| **Hierarchy** | Huge hero headline ("Wear your **CORNER** of *Yale.*") mixing outlined varsity type and pink script. Numbered section headers (**01** Find your people / **02** Born at Yale SOM / **03** Fan favourites). Chunky "pressable" buttons with a 3D offset shadow. | One message per screen, read in order. The press-down buttons feel tactile and invite clicking. |
| **Motion** | A pink **"Boola Boola" ticker** under the nav (pauses on hover). A spinning **"Born at Yale SOM" sticker**. Sections **reveal on scroll**. Cards **tilt toward the cursor**. Buttons press down. **Fly-to-bag**: the product photo arcs into the bag icon, which bounces. All motion turns off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. | Movement gives the page life without getting in the way. Fly-to-bag confirms the action instantly and points to where the bag is, which encourages a second add. |
| **Product presentation** | Cards are **swing tags**: a varsity category label (HOODIE / TEE…), a black price tag with a punched hole that swings on hover, **size dots** XS–XXL (sold-out sizes struck through), and a pulsing **"Only 2 left in XL"** badge (only for real 1–2 unit stock). The product page has a pink swing-tag price, **hover-to-zoom** on the photo (zooms toward the cursor), and a cream backdrop. | Shoppers see price, category and *their size* before clicking, so fewer dead-end clicks. Scarcity badges are true (from the database) and create urgency honestly. Zoom lets them inspect the print, the thing people check most on merch. |
| **Browsing** | **Pennant filters** (Hoodies 27 · Crewnecks 28 · Tees 25 · Quarter-zips 11 · Jackets 8 · Long sleeve 2), plus **sort by price**. They use the clean categories from Problem 9. | One tap to the right rack, with live counts. The pennant shape makes a plain filter bar part of the brand. |
| **Chat feel** | Boola feels like a **dorm-room text thread**: a gradient header with a pulsing "● Online · checks live stock", a Labubu **avatar** beside each reply, gradient "sent" bubbles, a **paw-print typing indicator** (🐾🐾🐾 "Boola is checking the racks…"), a pulsing ring on the launcher, and a one-time **"Need a size or a gift idea? Ask me!"** teaser 4 seconds after arriving. | People trust and use a chat that feels like a person. The teaser and ring make the assistant discoverable, which is the biggest drop-off for AI shop helpers. More chats lead to more guided finds and more add-to-bags. |
| **Brand moments** | A taped **polaroid of Evans Hall** (Yale SOM) in the hero, the SOM logo in the nav, section 02 and the footer, a trio of **Labubus**, round **chenille patches** (RC / GS / YB / FAM) on the collection cards, and a giant outlined **"BOOLA BOOLA"** footer. | Gives the store a personality and a story ("born at Yale SOM"), so it feels worth exploring and coming back to. |

**Nothing broken:** every card (catalogue, chat results, home favourites) still opens `/products/:id`. The chat, bag, auth and page context work as before. The production build is clean and there are no console errors.
